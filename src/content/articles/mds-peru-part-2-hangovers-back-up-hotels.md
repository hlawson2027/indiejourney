*MDS Peru Diaries, Part 2. Photos: me and Tugce.*

In [Part 1](/stories/mds-peru-part-1-looking-for-my-limit/) I explained what the MDS is and why I went looking for my limit in the Peruvian desert. This is the story of getting there, and of a first day that nearly tested me before the race had even begun.

## Leaving was the hardest part

The timing wasn't ideal. The race started just after Thanksgiving, and I felt guilty about leaving my family straight after a holiday. So I changed my flight to give myself a bit more time at home with them.

More time with family meant a few more drinks than planned, which meant one thing: I flew to Peru with a hangover. Not the training plan I'd recommend.

The flight from JFK to Lima is about eight hours nonstop. The good news was that I used points, so the flight was free, and I used points for my extra hotel nights too. I carried most of my race kit with me on board because I was terrified of my bag getting lost. You can't replace a carefully packed desert kit at the last minute. In the end, the flight was no problem at all.

## Plan B saved the day

When I got to the official MDS hotel, NM Lima in San Isidro, my room wasn't ready yet. Luckily I'd booked a back-up hotel just in case, so I headed there to get some rest.

I ended up staying the night at my back-up, the **Serenzza Hotel** in La Victoria, Lima, and it was a great find: clean, comfortable and cheap, with breakfast included and a sauna in the room. After a long flight and a hangover, a proper night's sleep was exactly what I needed. I'd book it again in a heartbeat.

> **Where I stayed: Serenzza Hotel, Lima**
>
> - **Address:** Avenida Santa Catalina 695, La Victoria, about 5 km from central Lima
> - **Early check-in:** from 6am, which is perfect if you land early off an overnight flight
> - **Check-out:** by 12pm
> - **What you get:** a 3-star hotel with air-conditioned rooms, private bathrooms, free Wi-Fi, a restaurant, a garden and terrace, free parking and a 24-hour front desk
> - **Why I'd recommend it:** good value, very clean, breakfast included and a sauna in my room. A great choice for a night or two before an MDS race or a trip around Peru.
> - **Money tip:** if you're a tourist, show your passport and immigration card at check-in to avoid Peru's 18% hotel tax (IVA).

## The welcome that didn't feel very welcoming

After a good rest, I headed back to NM Lima. MDS puts you in a shared room with another runner. I'd asked a few times for a single room, but no luck. When I checked in, my roommate hadn't arrived yet.

Then came the welcome meeting, and this is where it got hard. MDS is a French company, and a lot of the runners are French. Almost everything around me was in French, and I suddenly felt completely out of my depth.

I remember thinking: *is this it? Is this my fear?* Not the desert or the distance, but the fear of not fitting in and being left out.

I stayed for the welcome dinner anyway, hoping we'd get some information about the race. I went up to a table of women and asked if I could join them. They looked confused, said they didn't speak English, and told me there was no room, even though there were plenty of empty seats. Awkward.

I found an empty table. A woman sat down with me, then got up again to join her friends. It felt like there were cliques everywhere that I didn't know about. It was a feeling I knew well from other parts of my life: being the outsider who can't quite get in.

So I made a decision. Fine. I'd do this anyway, and I'd get my medal.

## And then, my people

Then two people walked over and asked if they could sit with me. I was so relieved. They were warm and welcoming, more people joined us, and suddenly our table was full of great people.

That's when I learned how these events tend to work: the French runners often stick together, and the rest of the world forms its own group. I found it odd at the time, and honestly I still do.

The dinner itself wasn't much. There was some pasta and a bit of food, and no real information about the race. I later found out lots of people eat out instead because the food isn't great. But I'm glad I stayed, because that dinner is where I met some wonderful people.

One of them was Zuzanna, a smart woman from Poland who lives in Switzerland. She'd done an MDS before and shared what these events are really like, which was exactly what I needed.

## Ice cream in Miraflores

After dinner, a group of us went for a walk around Miraflores, Lima's seaside neighbourhood. It felt safe and modern, like a cool version of Williamsburg. We stopped at a grocery store, bought ice cream and just chatted. It was the most normal, lovely end to a strange evening.

Travel tip: getting around Lima is easy and cheap. Ubers in Peru cost very little, so we used them everywhere.

## Meeting my Turkish princess

Back at the hotel, I was relaxing when my roommate arrived: Tugce, from Turkey and living in France. She was so warm, loving and caring. We started chatting straight away, like we'd known each other for years.

Before bed we'd made a plan to head into the centre of Lima the next day to pick up a few last bits for the race.

The day I thought might break me ended with new friends, ice cream and the best roommate I could have asked for. Day one: a success.
