I've had some crazy ideas, but flying to Chicago, picking up my bib, running a marathon and flying home again, all in about 48 hours and all on my own, might be one of my best. I ran it for charity, and I filmed the whole thing. Here's how it went (the full video is below).

## The plan, and the problem

The plan was simple. Fly out the day before the race, get to the expo, pick up my bib, sleep, run, fly home.

The problem was the bib. If I didn't make it to Chicago in time for packet pickup, there would be no bib, and no bib means no marathon. So from the moment I woke up, I was on the clock.

I'd been stressed the night before because I just had a feeling something was going to go wrong. It did. My flight was delayed.

## 3:30 a.m. and no ride

At half past three in the morning, I was standing outside waiting for a Lyft that said it would arrive in 12 minutes. Twelve minutes later, it still said 12 minutes. The price had been about $57, so I cancelled and checked Uber, which wanted around $150. I kept refreshing, the prices came back down to about $90, and finally a driver was five minutes away.

He got me to the airport, and by the end of the ride he'd promised to start going to the gym, give up junk food and run the next marathon with me. I'm holding him to that.

## Five hours in a lounge

I made it to the airport, but the flight was still delayed, so I settled into the lounge for the next five hours. Free food and drink took the edge off. Eventually we boarded, and I made it to Chicago.

## Racing the expo clock

From the airport I took the train into the city and went straight to the expo. I made it. Bib in hand, I celebrated the only way that makes sense the day before a marathon: pizza and Gatorade.

Then it was back to the hotel to work out the morning. My first plan was a shuttle to Midway and the Orange Line train toward Grant Park, but I wasn't sure how busy the trains and buses would be on race morning.

## Race morning

My alarm went off before 5 a.m. Here's what I wore and carried:

- A lightweight Adidas top and my running shorts
- My MP3 player for music
- Safety pins for my bib
- A running pack on my back, with water and snacks in the front pockets
- My phone and passport, since I was flying home straight after
- My race-day shoes

I tried to go a bit more lightweight than usual, and I'd recommend that for any destination race. Carry what you need, and nothing you'll resent at mile 20.

In the end I took an Uber instead of the train, got dropped off a little way from the road closures, and enjoyed a quiet 15-minute walk through Chicago in the dark. It was ten to six, and the race didn't start until 8:30, so there was plenty of time. I checked my bag and headed to Corral K.

## The race: flat, they said

Everyone tells you Chicago is flat and fast. I kept finding hills. And it was hot, unbelievably hot for October.

With about 8 km to go I was tired and it was taking me a long time, but I was still going. That's the thing about marathons: you don't have to feel good, you just have to keep moving.

And I finished. Chicago Marathon complete.

## Then straight back to the airport

No lingering, no long brunch. I collected my things and rushed back to the airport, and made it. Chicago, door to door, in about 48 hours.

## What I learned

- **Build in buffer.** Flights get delayed and rides don't show. If your bib pickup has a deadline, give yourself more time than you think you need.
- **Have a plan B for race morning.** I switched from the train to an Uber at the last minute, and getting dropped off a short walk from the closures made it easy.
- **Pack light and smart.** A small running pack with water, snacks and your essentials means you're ready for anything, including a dash to the airport.
- **Respect the weather.** A "flat, fast" course can still be hot and hard. Adjust your pace and keep drinking.
- **Doing it solo is more than OK.** Traveling and racing on your own is a little nerve-wracking and completely worth it.
