Every home has one: the awkward corner that collects laundry baskets and Amazon boxes. After 25 years as a designer, my favourite trick is to stop fighting that corner and give it one job. Make it the place you go to read.

You don't need a renovation or a new armchair. If you already have a chair (or can borrow one from the dining table or the bedroom), you can make the whole space feel intentional for **under $200**. Here's how I'd do it, step by step, with every piece from Walmart.

## First, find your corner

Look for a spot with a wall at your back and, ideally, a window to one side for daytime light. A corner of the bedroom, the end of a landing, or the dead space beside a sofa all work. The nook only needs to fit a chair, something small to put your tea on, and a bit of wall.

## Step 1: Light it (the most important step)

A reading nook lives or dies on its lighting. Overhead lights are too harsh and too far away, so bring the light down to where the book is.

**A wall sconce** is my favourite cheat for a nook, because it looks built-in. This one is cordless and rechargeable, with a scalloped shade and a remote, so there's no electrician and no wiring. Hang it about a foot above your shoulder, just to the side of the chair, so it lights the page without glaring in your eyes.

**A small portable lamp** adds a second, softer glow. I love a rechargeable lamp because you can carry it to the bath or the bedside afterwards. The milk glass shade gives a warm, candle-like light.

[[product: milk-glass-rechargeable-table-lamp]]

## Step 2: Soften it

The difference between "a chair in a corner" and "a nook" is texture. Two good pillows make even a hard dining chair comfortable for an hour of reading. These linen-look ones are a neutral beige, so they work with almost any chair.

[[product: linen-blend-pillows-2-pack]]

**Designer tip:** add a chunky knit throw over the arm or back of the chair. I've left room in the budget for one (see the breakdown below), so pick a colour you love. Rust, oatmeal or sage all look lovely next to the brass and rattan here.

## Step 3: Give your tea somewhere to land

You need a surface within arm's reach for a mug, your glasses and your phone (face down!). No room for a side table? Use a stool, an upturned crate or a stack of big books, and set **a riser tray** on top so it looks deliberate and keeps everything together.

[[product: natural-wood-riser-tray]]

Then add **flameless candles** to the tray. They give you the cosy flicker of candlelight with nothing to blow out if you doze off mid-chapter.

[[product: led-flameless-fluted-candle-set-2-pc]]

## Step 4: Make room for the books

A reading nook should hold your current read and the next two or three. **Bookends** on a windowsill, a narrow shelf or the floor beside the chair keep a small stack tidy. These gold and rattan ones are pretty enough to be decor in their own right.

[[product: gold-and-rattan-decorative-bookends]]

**A lidded box** hides the clutter that always ends up in a reading spot: reading glasses, bookmarks, a charging cable, the TV remote someone left there.

[[product: rattan-and-wood-decorative-box]]

## Step 5: Finish the wall

One piece of art pulls the whole corner together and tells your eye "this is a room within the room". A soft green floral print on linen is calm enough for a reading spot and ties in with any plants nearby. Hang it so the bottom of the frame sits about 8 to 10 inches above the sconce or the chair back.

## Make it yours

The beauty of a nook is that it's small, so you can change it with the seasons for very little. Swap the pillow covers, move the lamp, or turn the tray into a mini holiday display in December. The bones stay the same: good light, something soft, a place to land, your books, and one thing on the wall you love looking at.
