I've run the New York and Chicago marathons and ultras in Peru and the French Alps, and I'll be honest: the hardest runs aren't the long ones. They're the 6am runs in January, when it's dark, it's freezing and the bed is very warm.

Winter is where a lot of runners lose the fitness they built all year. It doesn't have to be that way. With the right layers and a few tricks, winter running can be some of the most peaceful running you'll do all year.

![A quiet frosty road lined with white trees on a winter morning](/uploads/winter-frosty-road.jpg)

## Dress for 10 degrees warmer

This is the golden rule. If you're cosy when you step out of the door, you're overdressed. You'll warm up in the first mile, so dress as if it's about 10°F (5–6°C) warmer than the thermometer says. Feeling slightly chilly for the first five minutes is exactly right.

## Tricks for getting out of the door

- **Lay your kit out the night before**, right down to the gloves. In the morning, you just put it on.
- **Warm up indoors.** Five minutes of leg swings, lunges and skipping in the hallway means you start the run warm.
- **Run into the wind first**, so it's at your back on the way home when you're sweaty.
- **Have a run buddy.** It's much harder to cancel on a friend than on yourself.
- **Get changed straight away** when you get back. Damp clothes chill you fast.
- **Let the pace go.** Cold muscles, layers and slippy pavements make you slower. That's fine. Winter is for consistency, not personal bests.

## When to stay inside

Ice is the one thing I don't mess with. If the pavements are sheet ice, it's a treadmill day, a cross-training day or a rest day. A missed run costs nothing; a broken wrist costs weeks.
