The New York City Marathon is one of the hardest races in the world to get into. Most people enter the lottery and hope. I didn't want to leave it to luck, so I took the long way round: the NYRR 9+1 program.

It got me to the start line of the **2026 TCS New York City Marathon**, and I've now done it all over again, so I'm **guaranteed a place in 2027** too. If you live anywhere near New York, this is the most reliable way in, and here's everything I've learned.

## What 9+1 actually is

9+1 is a program run by New York Road Runners (NYRR), the nonprofit that organizes the NYC Marathon. The deal is simple:

- **Run nine qualifying NYRR races** in one calendar year
- **Volunteer at one qualifying NYRR event** in that same year
- **Keep your NYRR membership active**

Do all of that and you earn guaranteed entry to the *next* year's marathon. So the races I ran in 2025 got me into the 2026 marathon, and my 2026 races got me into 2027.

A few things that surprised me:

- **There's no qualifying time.** You don't have to be fast. You just have to start and finish nine qualifying races.
- **"Guaranteed" doesn't mean free.** You still pay the marathon entry fee. You've earned the right to register, not a free bib.
- **The volunteer shift counts just as much as the races.** Nine races with no volunteer shift doesn't qualify you.

## How I did it

The key was treating it like a training plan rather than a list of races. NYRR races range from a single mile to half marathons, and they're spread across the year and all five boroughs. That gave me a reason to get out the door all year, and a ready-made build-up to the marathon itself.

### My favorite race: one wet mile

Of all my races, my favorite was the shortest: the New Balance Fifth Avenue Mile, a straight shot down 20 blocks of Fifth Avenue. It was a wet, rainy morning, and I ran my best mile ever: **8 minutes 38 seconds**.

There was no time to celebrate. As soon as I crossed the line I had to rush off and jump on a plane to Germany for work. Still soaked, still grinning. That's pretty much the story of my life in one morning.

### My +1: the day before the marathon

For my volunteer shift, I helped hand out runners' bags the day before the 2025 TCS New York City Marathon, and it turned out to be one of my favorite days of the whole year.

Every person who came through was running the marathon the next morning. Some were buzzing with excitement, some were scared, and plenty were just plain nervous. I loved chatting with them, wishing them luck and cheering them on. It's a reminder that every runner on that bridge has a story, and a lot of nerves the night before. A year later, I was one of them.

Doing 9+1 a second time was easier because I knew the rhythm: plan early, register as soon as races open, and book the volunteer shift before it's gone.

## My tips if you want to try it

**1. Plan your whole year up front.** Look at the NYRR race calendar as soon as it's published and pencil in more than nine races. Life happens: you'll get sick, travel, or have a family thing on race day. A couple of spares takes the pressure off.

**2. Register the moment races open.** 9+1 has become so popular that qualifying races fill up fast. Know when registration opens and be ready.

**3. Book your volunteer shift early, and pick one you'll enjoy.** Volunteer spots can be even harder to get than race entries. Check that the shift is marked as eligible for 9+1, and book one on a day you're not racing. If you can, volunteer around the marathon itself: meeting the runners the day before is something special.

**4. Make sure each race actually counts.** Not every NYRR event is a qualifier, and some races now set aside bibs that don't count toward 9+1. Check before you register.

**5. Use the races as training.** Short races are great speed work, and the longer ones make perfect long-run days in a marathon block. By the time November came round, I'd raced all over the city.

**6. Keep your membership active.** It sounds obvious, but a lapsed membership can cost you your spot.

## What's changing: the new 9+1 drawing

This is the big one if you're planning for 2028. NYRR has changed the program because demand got so high that runners struggled to get into races at all.

- **From the 2027 program year, you need to be picked in a drawing first.** The drawing is free to enter for NYRR members. For this first round, applications ran from September 17 to 29, 2026, with the drawing on October 1.
- **15,000+ members are selected.** Those runners pay $99 to become "9+1 Passholders," which includes a race selection process to help them get into the races and volunteer slots they need.
- **Then it's the same 9+1:** nine qualifying races and one volunteer shift during 2027, for guaranteed entry to the 2028 marathon (you still pay the entry fee).
- **If you're already working toward 2027 entry, nothing changes.** Runners who completed 9+1 in 2026, like me, still earn their 2027 place under the old rules.

So if you want to run New York in 2028 through 9+1, watch for the next drawing announcement from NYRR, and make sure your membership is active by their deadline.

## Is it worth it?

For me, absolutely. Nine races and a volunteer shift sounds like a lot, but it turned a single race into a whole year of running in the city I live near, with a guaranteed spot at the end. And crossing the finish line in Central Park after a year of working for it felt even better than winning a lottery ever could.

See you on the Verrazzano Bridge in 2027.

*Rules and dates change, so always check the latest details on NYRR's website before you plan your year.*
