*MDS Peru Diaries, Part 1. All photos in this series were taken by me and my friend Tugce.*

Eleven months before the MDS 120 Peru, I wasn't a runner at all. In that short time I'd run races all over New York, completed the NYRR 9+1 (nine qualifying races plus one volunteer shift, which earns you a place in the New York City Marathon) and run the Brooklyn Half. I was hooked, and I kept wondering where my limit was. I hadn't found one yet: no wall that made me stop, and no fear that made me turn back.

So I went looking for it. In a desert. In Peru.

The New York and Chicago marathons, and the ultra in the French Alps, all came later. This is where it began.

This is the first post in a series about my week at the MDS 120 Peru in December 2025. This one covers the what and the why. Part 2 is the chaotic journey there, and from Part 3 we're in the desert.

## First, what is the MDS?

MDS stands for Marathon des Sables, the "marathon of the sands." It's a French company, and the story goes that it exists because founder Patrick Bauer once crossed the Moroccan desert alone, on foot. The first race was held in 1986, and it's widely seen as the event that invented multi-day desert stage racing.

The big one is **MDS The Legendary**: around 250 km over six stages in the Sahara, carrying everything you need on your back. It celebrated its 40th edition in 2026.

Over the years, MDS has grown into a whole family of adventures:

- **MDS 120 events:** 70, 100 or 120 km (you choose) over 3 stages, spread across 4 days in the desert, as part of a 7-day trip. Locations have included Morocco (the Sahara and the Atlantic Coast), Fuerteventura, Wadi Rum in Jordan, Cappadocia in Turkey, and Peru.
- **MDS Trek:** a walking version in Morocco with a more comfortable camp and no need to carry all your food.
- **MDS Handi:** an adapted format so people with disabilities can take part.
- **MDS Raid Namibia** and **MDS Crazy Loops** in France, which add new kinds of terrain to the mix.

The golden rule at almost all of them is **self-sufficiency**. You carry your own food, clothes, sleeping bag and kit for the whole race. The organisers give you rationed water and a tent to sleep in, and that's it.

## Why Peru?

MDS Peru takes place in the Ica Desert on Peru's south coast, finishing in the Paracas National Reserve, and it doesn't run every year. It's usually held once every three years, and 2025 was the 4th edition and its return after three years away, which made it feel like a now-or-never chance. (Good news if this series tempts you: after high demand, MDS is bringing Peru back early, in 2027.)

The Ica Desert isn't just a sea of soft sand. It's a mix of hard-packed plains, rocky ridges and huge dunes, and parts of the course run right down to the Pacific Ocean. Running through the desert and finishing beside the Pacific is what makes Peru so special.

## How the race works

The 2025 race ran from 29 November to 6 December. The week looks like this: arrive in Peru, check in, pass the kit and medical checks, travel out to the desert, run three stages, then come back to civilisation for a hot shower and a medal.

The three stages:

- **Stage 1:** about 25 km (my watch said 25.72 km)
- **Stage 2:** the long one, 20, 40 or 60+ km depending on your distance. There were three different routes, one for each distance, and you choose yours at a checkpoint during the stage. The longest route in 2025 was 62.9 km, with an 18-hour time limit. I took the shortest, which still came out at 30.4 km on my watch.
- **Stage 3:** about 25 km, to the finish

You can run or walk it at your own pace. Between stages you sleep at the bivouac, a little village of yellow MDS tents in the sand.

## What I carried

Everything I'd need for four days went into one 20-litre pack. Lining it all up on the hotel bed made it feel very real.

- **Food:** freeze-dried meals (Peak Refuel and Backpacker's Pantry), Maurten gels, electrolyte sachets, bars, jerky, pretzels and a few treats.
- **Clothes:** running tops, shorts, leggings, socks and a warm layer for the cold desert nights.
- **Care kit:** sunscreen, anti-chafe balm, plasters, pain relief, earplugs, wipes and tissues.
- **Mandatory kit:** a small Esbit stove and mug, a compass, a knife, a spork, a survival blanket, safety pins and a mirror.

## Why MDS, and how I got ready

Before I signed up, I watched videos of past MDS events. Everything looked so well put together, from the camps to the safety team, and that gave me the confidence to go for it.

My training was simple: lots of running and lots of walking. I ran on the beach near my home to get used to sand, and I went on long walks carrying a weighted pack so my body knew what four days with a loaded bag would feel like.

The hardest part was my knee. It had been a real issue for months, and I ended up wearing a knee support for the whole event. It held up, though, as you'll find out, my feet were a different story.

> **What it cost**
>
> - **Entry:** MDS 120 entries are priced in euros, and your entry includes your race bib and four hotel nights in a shared room. Check marathondessables.com for current prices. Registration for MDS 120 Peru 2027 opens on 9 December 2026.
> - **Payment plan:** I paid in instalments, which made it much more manageable. For other recent MDS 120 events, card payments could be split into 1, 3 or 5 instalments, but the options depend on when you register.
> - **Flights and extra hotels:** I used points for my flight and the extra hotel nights.
> - **Getting around:** Ubers in Peru are very cheap, so there's no need to budget much for transport.
> - **Kit:** the biggest variable. My full kit list is in [What I Packed](/stories/mds-peru-what-i-packed/).

## Ready for a challenge

I didn't sign up because I thought it would be easy. I signed up because I genuinely didn't know what would happen, and that was the point. Was there a limit in there somewhere? A fear I hadn't met yet?

I was about to find out, though not quite in the way I expected. It started before I'd even got on the plane.
