When I signed up to run the **2026 Bank of America Chicago Marathon**, I didn't just want a medal. I wanted the miles to mean something. So I ran for **Special Children's Charities**, a Chicago charity that supports children and adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities and works to build more inclusive opportunities and communities.

My goal was **$2,500**. And I made it. Here's exactly how, including the part that flopped, in case you're fundraising for a race of your own.

![The Chicago skyline with low clouds drifting between the skyscrapers](/uploads/chicago-skyline-clouds.jpg)

## 1. I started at the top

The first person I asked was the **owner of the company I work for**. Then the **executive team**. Then my **coworkers**.

This order matters. When people see that the boss has already given, it makes it easier for everyone else to say yes, and it sets the tone for how much people give.

## 2. I shared it on LinkedIn

Next, I posted about it on **LinkedIn**. People who know you professionally are often happy to support a personal goal, especially one with a clear finish line and a good cause behind it.

## 3. I baked

This is where it got fun.

- **I made trifles and sold them at work.** Nobody can resist a trifle (I'm English, after all).
- **I made cakes and sweet bags for my daughter to take to school**, and sold those too.

Bake sales won't raise thousands on their own, but they keep the fundraiser in front of people, and every sale adds up.

## 4. What didn't work: 100 letters

I sent out **100 letters to local companies** asking them to sponsor me.

It didn't work.

I'm sharing this because it's useful to know. Cold letters to businesses that don't know you are easy to ignore. Personal asks, from you, to people who know you, work far better.

## 5. A challenge prize got me over the line

Then something lovely happened. In September I won an Amazon Influencer challenge run by Data Coach Claire, and part of the prize was **$500**. I asked Claire to donate it straight to my fundraiser for the final stretch. (You can read more about that challenge in [How I've Earned $2,878 in Commissions So Far](/stories/my-affiliate-income-journey-so-far/).)

Finally, I **donated some myself**, and the page hit its goal: **$2,500, fully funded**.

![The Chicago River between skyscrapers, with bridges crossing the water](/uploads/chicago-river-bridges.jpg)

## My fundraising tips

- **Ask the most senior or most generous person first.** Their donation sets the bar.
- **Make it personal.** Tell people why you're running and who it helps.
- **Use what you're good at.** For me, that was baking.
- **Don't rely on cold letters.** Spend that time on personal asks instead.
- **Keep sharing updates.** Training runs, milestones and "we're 80% there!" posts remind people to give.
- **Put your own money in too.** It shows you believe in it.
