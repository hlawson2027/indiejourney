Welcome to The Spain Diaries. If you're new here, this is the place to start, because everything else on this site, every side job, every story and every shop, points back to one dream: **my husband and I are moving to Spain.**

We're not there yet. This is the story of how we're getting there, with the real numbers, the wins and the slow bits. I hope you'll come along for the journey.

## A dream that started over 20 years ago

When we moved to America over 20 years ago, Spain was already the dream. Even then we talked about one day living somewhere warm and slow, with long lunches and sunshine nearly every day.

We also knew it wouldn't just happen. To do it properly, without stress and without worrying about money, we'd need to **work hard and save hard** for a long time. So that's what we've done.

It's also the honest answer to a question I get a lot: *why do you have so many jobs?* I've been a designer for over 25 years. I write books under three author names. We're landlords in the UK and Chicago. I run affiliate shops and online stores. Every one of those is a brick in the house we're building in Spain.

## Why Spain?

### The weather (compared to England, it's not even close)

I grew up in England, so I know grey. London gets roughly **1,400 hours of sunshine a year**. Málaga, on the Costa del Sol, gets around **2,900 to 3,000**, about twice as much. In January, a typical day in Málaga reaches about 17°C, while London is around 8°C.

After a lifetime of drizzle and a few New York winters, I'm ready to swap the umbrella for sunglasses.

### Life costs a lot less than in New York

This is the big one. Using Málaga as an example, cost-of-living comparisons put a family's monthly costs there at **about $4,000, compared with about $9,200 in New York City**, and family rent at **around $1,700 compared with $4,400**. Groceries, eating out and everyday bills all come in well under what we pay now.

In plain terms, **our money goes more than twice as far.** The same budget that keeps us afloat in New York could give us a genuinely comfortable life in Spain, with room left over for travel and the people we love.

### Houses are so much cheaper

Spanish homes cost a fraction of what we'd pay in New York, and that changes everything. Instead of a 30-year mortgage, we can aim to **buy our house outright**. No mortgage means no monthly payment hanging over us, which is the kind of freedom we've been working towards all along.

### Europe on our doorstep

From Spain, so much of Europe is a short, cheap flight or a train ride away. Lisbon and Porto. Paris, Rome and the Greek islands. The French Alps, where I've run an ultramarathon and would happily go back. Weekends that would be a whole vacation from New York become a quick trip. (If you want a taste, my [Lisbon blue and lemon table lookbook](/lookbooks/lisbon-blue-and-lemon-table/) is basically my daydream on a plate.)

### Being closer to family

This matters more to me than the sunshine. Our daughter is going to university in England, and much of our family is there too. From New York, seeing them means a long, expensive trip. From Spain, England is a two- or three-hour flight. We'll be able to pop over for a birthday, or have our daughter come down for the holidays without it being a huge production.

### The laid-back life

And then there's the life itself. The pace we've been dreaming of:

- **Afternoon naps.** The siesta is real, and I fully intend to take part.
- **Pool time.** Long, lazy afternoons with a book and nowhere to be.
- **Snack time.** Tapas, olives, fresh bread and a cold drink in the shade.
- **Quality time.** Long dinners with my husband, visits from our daughter, and our cat Jinx finding the sunniest spot in the house.

After 20-plus years of hustling, we want a life that's slower and fuller.

## The plan, in numbers

I promised real numbers, so here they are.

**1. Buy our house outright.** We're **50% of the way there** on our house savings. Because homes in Spain cost so much less than in New York, this goal is actually within reach.

**2. Bring in $4,000 a month.** To live in Spain as non-EU residents we need a visa, and that means showing a steady monthly income. The common route for people who aren't working for a Spanish employer is the non-lucrative visa, which for 2026 asks a couple to show about **€3,000 a month**. Our target is **$4,000 a month**, so we're comfortably above the minimum with a cushion for real life.

**3. Grow our passive income.** This is where all those extra jobs come in. Right now our Amazon affiliate income brings in **about $800 a month**. That's 20% of the way to our $4,000 goal, from something I built in my spare time. Next I'm growing:

- **Walmart** affiliate finds
- **Zazzle**, my art and design store
- **Other affiliate programs** that fit what I already love sharing: running gear, home, travel and gifts
