*MDS Peru Diaries, Part 5. Photos: me and Tugce.*

In [Part 4](/stories/mds-peru-part-4-blisters-sand-mountains/) I survived the sand mountain, a sandstorm and the medical tent, then spent rest day ignoring the doctor and wading into the Pacific. Now there was one stage left.

## Just keep moving

My feet were in a bad state on the last day. There was no way I could run. But I knew one thing: I just had to keep moving.

The stage started at 5am in the dark, with head torches on, and it was fun. Tugce and I got separated right at the start because she had to stop and take off a layer. But we kept bumping into each other along the way.

![Camp in the dark before the start](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8325.jpg)

![Walking into the fog on the final stage](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8326.jpg)

I had my playlist, heavy on Fleetwood Mac, and the most beautiful views. Honestly, I wish I could have run, because the course was great. The faster runners started later, so some of my friends came past me during the stage, which was a lot of fun. Your name is printed on your bib, so people cheer you on as they pass.

![The trail running down to the bay](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8328.jpg)

## Me and my thoughts

It was interesting to do part of this journey on my own, just me and my thoughts.

I've lived in the USA for 20 years, and for most of that time I've felt different, more different than normal. Something Tugce said had stuck with me: it wasn't me, it was my environment. People in the USA are very different from people in the UK, and the way I grew up was so different that for years it was hard to relate.

Out there, walking through the desert, I realised I was OK. Just as I am. It was overwhelming. Back in [Part 2](/stories/mds-peru-part-2-hangovers-back-up-hotels/) I'd wondered if my fear was not fitting in. Now I knew there was nothing wrong with me.

On one of the days, Tugce and I had talked about leaving things in the desert: any problems or worries, you leave them there. So I did. I left the idea that I was different right there in the sand.

I thought about my husband too, and how much I love him. He was the one who told me to do this. It's my life, and I don't need anyone's permission to live it. I love that.

The whole time, my playlist felt perfectly in tune with my thoughts.

The desert didn't break me. It made me.

## A Coke in the desert

As I carried on, I spotted a sign. It was a little shop, in the middle of the desert. I thought: they have to have Coca-Cola. I stopped, and they did!

After missing out on the rest-day Cola, I finally got my Coke in the desert. Everyone at the finish was amazed and wanted to know how I'd got it. Peru's food is amazing, but that Coke and the orange on rest day were honestly the best things I tasted all trip.

![Me, my Coke and the shopkeeper](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8329.jpg)

Further on I passed a whale jawbone skeleton and other skeletons in the sand, which was really cool. And the scenery just got better and better as the route entered the Paracas National Reserve and ran along the coast.

![The Paracas National Reserve sign](/uploads/mds-peru-img_3927.jpg)

![Dunes, cliffs and deep blue water on the Paracas coast](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8332.jpg)

## The finish line

Then I turned a corner and saw the "Last KM" flags, and the finish line below.

![The Last KM flags above the bay](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8340.jpg)

![The final stretch along the beach](/uploads/mds-peru-img_3945.jpg)

Then I saw Yuk, my friend from Hong Kong, running towards me. She'd stayed at the finish to watch me and our other friends come in, and she ran alongside me. Then I heard someone shout my name: Shita, my friend from India. We ran to the finish line together, and waiting for us at the finish was Tugce.

It was truly a wonderful feeling, completing the journey and doing it with my new lifelong friends.

![The four of us with our medals at the MDS 120 finish arch](/uploads/mds-peru-img_1719.jpg)

> **Stage three in numbers (4 December 2025)**
>
> - **Start:** 5am, in the dark
> - **Distance:** 27.29 km
> - **Moving time:** 6 hours
> - **Average pace:** 13:13 per km, my fastest of the week, on my worst feet
> - **Climbing:** 626 m, with two big climbs to about 215 m near the start and the end, and flatter coastline in between
> - **Calories burned:** 1,504
>
> **My whole MDS 120 Peru, by my watch**
>
> - **Distance:** 83.4 km over three stages
> - **Moving time:** about 23 hours 17 minutes
> - **Climbing:** 2,218 m
> - **Calories burned:** 5,351

![My stage three route along the Paracas coast](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8520.jpg)

## Did I find my limit?

I went to Peru looking for my limit and wondering if I'd meet a fear I hadn't found yet. My feet found their limit, that's for sure. But I kept moving.

The fear I found wasn't about the desert at all. It was the fear of not fitting in. And the desert is where I finally put it down.

People sometimes ask me my finish time. It doesn't matter. Completing it does.
