*MDS Peru Diaries, Part 3. Photos: me and Tugce.*

In [Part 2](/stories/mds-peru-part-2-hangovers-back-up-hotels/) I survived a hangover, an awkward welcome dinner and a lot of French, and ended the night with new friends and the best roommate I could have asked for. Now it was time to get ready for the desert.

## Day two: shopping, lunch and the big kit check

Tugce and I headed out to pick up a few last bits. I got a new T-shirt, and Tugce found a bikini and a few other treats. Where we were felt very modern, more like a stylish city neighbourhood than anything I'd pictured before I arrived.

Then we had lunch, and I have to talk about the food. Peruvian food is fantastic. Everything was so fresh, and it reminded me of the food I've had in Mexico. My favourites were a lovely beef stew and a really tasty ceviche. If you ever go, eat everything.

![Breakfast in the tiled courtyard at NM Lima](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8083.jpg)

![Tugce at the Miraflores clifftop](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8106.jpg)

Back at the hotel it was time for the official kit check. MDS goes through your bag and makes sure you have every item on the mandatory list. You're carrying your whole life on your back for four days, so they need to know you can look after yourself out there.

> **The MDS 120 mandatory kit list**
>
> - A 20-litre race pack (like the WAA Ultra Bag 20L) and the ability to carry 1.5 litres of water
> - A sleeping bag
> - A head torch, switched on from dusk, plus spare batteries or a power bank
> - 10 safety pins
> - A lighter and a whistle
> - A knife with a metal blade
> - Skin antiseptic
> - A signalling mirror and a survival blanket
> - 8 stock cubes (two a day), which replace salt tablets
> - Sunscreen
> - €100 in cash and your passport
> - A mobile phone and external battery
> - A foot-care kit for treating your own blisters
> - Food: at least 2,000 calories for every day of the race
>
> Always check the official list for your own race, as it can change.

We met back up with Zuzanna, then did one last bag check of our own in the room. Playlists sorted. Early night. The adventure was about to begin.

## The bus south

We were up early for the bus, and everyone was handed a little lunch bag of food for the journey. The drive from Lima to Ica alone is around five hours, and then we kept going, out into the desert. By the time we finally started running, it was after 11 in the morning.

We stopped at a petrol station along the way, and the views out of the window were amazing. What struck me most was how different the rest of Peru was from the city we'd just left. Modern Lima gave way to colourful little towns, homes stacked up on dusty hillsides, bright green fields with mountains behind them, and red tuk-tuks buzzing around Ica.

Then the road ran out. The bus drove off into the desert, onto the sand, and stopped. The event was ready.

Was I?

We piled off the bus, crouched in the dirt to fill our bottles and strap everything onto our packs one last time, then joined the crowd heading towards the big red MDS 120 start arch. Bib 219, with a little Union Jack on it. This was really happening.

## Stage one: magic, and the last two in the race

Tugce and I decided to stick together, and I'm so glad we did. The first day was magical. Great vibes and incredible views, with nothing but sand, sky and the odd stubborn desert plant.

Early on there was a big climb, up to the highest point of the day at around 8 km. Lots of people charged up it fast and then burned out. I was good: I kept going slow and steady, and it paid off. In the desert, slow and steady really does win.

![Tugce on the trail](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8208.jpg)

![A lone desert plant in the Ica Desert](/uploads/mds-peru-img_3256.jpg)

Along the route there are checkpoints. Each one has water to refill your bottles, and if you want, the team will pour water over you to cool you down. There are toilets too, but this is the desert, so they're little pop-up tents with a seat and a bag. MDS takes leaving the desert clean very seriously.

The people at the water stations were brilliant. They cheered every single runner in, as if we were winning the whole thing.

> **The MDS team: who's who**
>
> MDS runs on an army of staff and volunteers, and you can tell them apart by their jackets.
>
> - **Blue jackets** look after the runners: welcoming you, guiding you, handing out water, keeping the camp running and cheering you on.
> - **Orange jackets** are the medical team: doctors, nurses and podiatrists. They patch up your feet, check on you at checkpoints and in camp, and some even run the course backwards to find anyone who's struggling.
>
> I can't say enough good things about them. Every time I needed help, someone was there.

At one checkpoint we were chatting to the team, and they mentioned that one of the staff walks with the very last person in the race. Tugce and I looked around and realised: that was us. We were last! How did that happen? We burst out laughing and took off running.

We soon caught up with the other back-of-the-pack runners and carried on through the desert together.

## "It looks so close"

Then we saw it: the ocean, and the camp. We'd made it!

Except we hadn't. It looked close, but in the desert nothing is as close as it looks. We kept going, and going, until finally we were there.

We each had our own spot in the bivouac. I found mine and discovered my tentmates were from England, which was lovely. They'd been there for hours. These were fast runners. Tugce and I were more the team that stopped to enjoy the sights and soak in every bit of the experience, and I wouldn't change that.

![The yellow MDS tents at the bivouac](/uploads/mds-peru-img_3662.jpg)

> **Stage one in numbers (1 December 2025)**
>
> - **Distance:** 25.72 km
> - **Time:** 6 hours 38 minutes (6:31 of it moving)
> - **Average pace:** 15:14 per km
> - **Climbing:** 556 m of elevation gain, topping out at 775 m around the 8 km mark
> - **The finish:** the last few kilometres drop all the way down to sea level and the Pacific
> - **Calories burned:** 1,648

![My stage one route, from the desert down to the coast](/uploads/mds-peru-img_8516.jpg)

But our night wasn't done yet. There was dinner to cook and feet to take care of.
