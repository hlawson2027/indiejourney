*Photos: me.*

I earned my place in the 2026 TCS New York City Marathon [through NYRR's 9+1](/stories/how-i-got-into-the-nyc-marathon-with-9-plus-1/). Just three weeks after a [very hot Chicago Marathon](/stories/chicago-marathon-in-48-hours/), it was time to run my home city.

## Staying close to the start

My husband's idea was for me to stay close to the start, and it was a good one. I still needed a 30-minute Uber on race morning, but it worked out perfectly.

The best part? I was in Wave 5, which didn't start until 11:30 a.m. So I got to watch the start of the race on TV, in bed. Then I headed out.

## The start village

Then came the waiting. I lay out on the grass and chilled, then got myself a bagel and a Gatorade. Very New York.

## Hot again

And it was hot again. Not Chicago hot, but warm, sunny and still. Late waves run into the warmest part of the day, and starting at 11:30 meant I was out there right through the afternoon.

This time, though, I knew what I was in for. Within the first 10 km I could feel it, and I wasn't going to make the same mistakes. This time I had a cap from the start.

> **How warm was it?**
>
> - The afternoon high was forecast to reach about 63°F, which would make it one of the warmest NYC Marathon days of the past decade.
> - Early waves started closer to 50°F. Wave 5 started at 11:30 a.m. and ran into the warmest part of the day.
> - Clear skies and almost no wind meant full sun and very little to cool you down.

The crowds were brilliant, and the views were amazing: Brooklyn, Queens, the bridges, every borough.

## That foot again

Then my left leg and foot started to hurt again, just like in Chicago. This time it felt like something had snapped on the top of my foot. But I carried on.

## Finishing in the dark

By the time I reached the finish, it was dark. I got my medal and my orange poncho, and joined the river of orange walking out of Central Park.

![My 2026 NYC Marathon medal](/uploads/nyc-2026-img_8136.jpg)

![A sea of orange ponchos leaving the finish in the dark](/uploads/nyc-2026-img_8139.jpg)

## The bag-check rule to watch out for

I wasn't able to check a bag, because I hadn't selected that option with NYRR before race day. NYRR asks you to choose between checking a bag or the no-bag option, which gets you a poncho at the finish, and you have to choose ahead of time.

Honestly? I'm glad it worked out that way. No bag meant no queue at the end, and the poncho kept me warm.

## The long walk to Penn

Getting home was the hardest part. Getting an Uber or a cab to Penn Station was a nightmare, so I had to walk for 30 minutes. Not what you want after running a marathon.

## What I learned

- **Stay close to the start if you can.** It made race morning easy, even with a 30-minute Uber.
- **A late wave means a late finish.** Wave 5 started at 11:30 a.m. and I finished in the dark. Plan for it.
- **Bring a cap and respect the sun.** Even in November, the afternoon can be warm.
- **Choose bag check or poncho before race day.** Look out for NYRR's option when you confirm your entry. No bag means no queue.
- **Plan your way home.** Cabs and Ubers near the finish are hard to get. Be ready to walk.

Next up? I've already qualified for the 2027 NYC Marathon through 9+1 again.
