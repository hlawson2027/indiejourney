I love having people round at Christmas. What I don't love is spending the whole time in the kitchen and missing the party. Over the years I've worked out a way of hosting that's mostly done before anyone rings the doorbell, so I actually get to sit down with a glass of something.

Here's my plan, plus the few pieces that do the heavy lifting on the table.

![Walmart holiday hosting mood board: bamboo charcuterie board, gold candlesticks, striped napkins, tartan tablecloth, cake stand, stemless wine glasses, glass drinks dispenser, jute runner and white platter](/uploads/holiday-hosting-walmart-board.jpg)

## 1. Decide the menu, then cut it by a third

The biggest cause of hosting stress is doing too much. Pick one showstopper, then make everything else simple or bought. Nobody remembers the fourth side dish; they remember that you were relaxed and fun.

## 2. Set the table the night before

This is my number one tip. Lay the cloth, the runner, the plates, glasses, napkins and candles the evening before. It takes the pressure off the day, and you'll wake up to a table that already looks like a party.

A **tartan tablecloth** says Christmas straight away, and a **jute runner** on top softens it and makes it feel layered and considered.

## 3. Light candles everywhere

Candlelight forgives everything: the dust, the mismatched chairs, the slightly burnt roast potatoes. A set of **gold candlesticks** in different heights instantly makes the table look styled.

## 4. Swap the starter for a grazing board

Instead of a plated starter, put out a big board when people arrive. It can be built an hour ahead, people help themselves, and it keeps everyone happy while the main course finishes.

[[product: agesis-charcuterie-board-set]]

A **cake stand** isn't just for cake. Use it to lift cheese, mince pies or fruit so the table has different heights.

[[product: bhg-cake-stand]]

## 5. Let people serve themselves

Put the drinks in one place and let guests help themselves. A **glass drinks dispenser** filled with punch, sangria or lemony water means you're not topping up glasses all night.

[[product: glass-beverage-dispenser-wood-lid]]

**Stemless wine glasses** are my go-to for parties: they're harder to knock over and they go in the dishwasher.

[[product: bhg-stemless-wine-glasses-4-pack]]

A **big white platter** is the hardest-working piece in my cupboard. Everything looks better served on it, and it goes with every theme.

[[product: bhg-white-porcelain-serving-platter]]

If you need extra plates for a crowd, plain white stoneware is cheap, stacks well and works with any table.

[[product: mainstays-white-stoneware-dinnerware-12]]

## 6. Make a simple timeline

Write out the day backwards from when you want to eat: when the meat goes in, when the potatoes go in, when to take the board out. Stick it on the fridge. It frees your brain to talk to people.

## 7. Accept help

When someone asks "What can I bring?", give them a real answer: dessert, ice, a bottle of something. And when they offer to wash up, let them.
